# Independent local visual review, output02

Scope: original full-resolution local PNGs, plus a few frames extracted from complete local MP4s. No browser, network, public result, implementation, or deployment checks. These sampled stills do not establish continuous geometric safety or full temporal smoothness. No human approval is inferred.

## Cradle four normal

Inspected each before/after PNG independently at frames 15, 90, 228 in output02/images. Also extracted video frames 60 and 150 separately for each version to cradle-before-01/02.png and cradle-after-01/02.png in this directory. Native presentation is 1920x1080.

Conclusion: the Newton cradle identity is retained, but visibly weakened. Four circular bobs remain legible as suspended objects, paired strings remain connected to the top rail, adjacent middle bobs touch, and an outer bob separates from the row at selected times. The result still reads as a Newton cradle. This describes visible proportions rather than an implementation strategy. It has substantially less typographic prominence and a much more compressed suspension than before.

Main letters: the sampled lyric reads 風の先へ when all four letters have appeared. Before, letters are roughly 130px tall; after, approximately 36px. The letters are individually decipherable at native size, but occupy a small lower-right portion of a nearly empty 1080p composition. No sampled main letter is covered by another bob or by the support. Before frame15 and after frame15 both have a blank final bob during the reveal; that is preexisting timing, not an introduced missing glyph.

Support/rods: before has a tall rectangular stand and long visibly pendular suspension. After has a much shallower stand and very short visible strings above bobs. The paired strings remain visible and attached, but their stubby proportions weaken the sense of hanging and swinging. Strings pass in front of fading bobs in frame228 in both versions, a preexisting layering feature.

Spacing/motion sampled: before frame60 shows the first bob separated left; frame150 shows the fourth bob separated right. After equivalents retain that left/right phase pattern, with noticeably smaller displacement. After frame90 and frame150 have a clear gap to the right bob; three middle/left bobs remain closely aligned. These samples support retained swing ordering, but cannot establish full motion continuity or smoothness.

Overlap: both frame15 versions show cyan and pink trail circles behind the pale main bobs. After frame15 the right cyan trail rises above and intersects the stand's top rail; the compressed rods make trail/support interference more evident than before. This is an introduced compositional interaction, while multicolored trail overlap itself already exists before. Sampled main bobs remain distinguishable; no new main-letter occlusion observed.

Stage/cropping: after the entire cradle sits at bottom right, approximately x850–1770 and y759–1058 in the sampled images, leaving very large dark empty space above/left. Its base is close to the bottom edge but fully present in these samples. Before spans most of the screen and sits centrally. No sampled stage-edge cropping in either version. This placement change materially reduces visual prominence. It is not a continuous-safety certification.

Assessment: do not characterize this as visual parity with the original. Recognizable identity survives, but readability/prominence, hanging proportions, and swing emphasis are visibly reduced. Whether that tradeoff is acceptable requires an explicit product/user decision; numeric circle clearance alone cannot settle it.

## Fan four high motion

Inspected individual native-resolution before/after PNGs at frames15/90/228 and independently extracted video frames60/150 for each. The four-bob hanging fan identity is retained: common yellow pivot, fine strings, alternating pale/yellow circular bobs, and top-to-bottom lyric order 風の先へ. Strings still visibly meet bob contours. Later frames show changing lateral arrangement, with the final bob moving to opposite sides between sampled frames90 and150 in both versions. Full smoothness is not established.

After letters are smaller (about40px compared with about105px before) but readable at native1920x1080. The bobs are separated more clearly in the settled samples60/90/150, so middle glyphs have no sampled neighboring-bob occlusion. The effect now occupies a narrow strip near the right edge, leaving extensive dark space to the left. No sampled frame-edge clipping; the rightmost bob in frame90 remains visibly inside the stage.

Frame15 has a material readability problem in both versions: overlapping stacked entry bobs hide most of the third character and the fourth bob is blank. After preserves this preexisting reveal/overlap issue; it does not solve it. Cyan/pink trail circles overlap behind the first and intermediate bobs in both versions. Frame228 has colored glyph ghosts offset rightward, particularly on 先 and へ; these also exist before. The black main glyph remains readable after. Thin strings crossing over faded upper bobs at228 occur in both versions. No introduced disconnection, order reversal, or sampled main-letter loss observed.

Assessment: hanging fan identity and order survive; settled spacing is clear. Reduced type size and right-edge placement weaken prominence, while early-entry occlusion remains a source issue. No human approval inferred.

## Chunks one original run

Inspected each individual native-resolution before/after PNG at frames15/90/228 and extracted video frames60/150. Only one glyph, 風, appears throughout this chosen case; this review cannot assess multi-chunk ordering.

The hanging-paper identity remains clear: pale rounded tag/card, dark punched-hole mark, fine string, yellow pivot, and short horizontal support. The paper is now near square and larger in total area (roughly330x320px versus the original roughly275x190px). This accepted shape change does not look like a circle or an unrecognizable object. Main 風 remains clearly legible, although its strokes are smaller than the original and have more surrounding whitespace.

After support sits near x1438/y238 in sampled settled frames, paper around x1250–1620/y330–660. Before has pivot near top center and a much longer suspension down to a lower central paper. After the exposed string is about100px, a conspicuous shortening from the original600px-scale string. It remains plainly visible; the paper still looks suspended, though with substantially less pendulum length and visual swing emphasis.

String/paper/hole connection: both versions show the string reaching the paper's top contour, with the dark hole farther below on the paper. The visible string does not continue over the opaque paper to the hole. This apparent short gap between visible string tip and hole mark preexists; after does not introduce an obvious free-floating string or detached glyph. Main glyph remains centered on the paper without edge collisions.

Frame15 cyan/pink duplicate paper trails remain behind and above the main paper; after trails are shallower offsets and do not cover the black glyph. At228 subtle cyan/pink glyph ghosts remain beside the main glyph in both. Sampled rotations change sides at60/150/228 in the same broad pattern. No sampled stage clipping, paper/glyph collision, or trail-caused loss of legibility. Frame228 shows partial fading in both versions. Entry and end frames outside these samples were not visually checked, so no complete entry/exit claim.

Assessment: recognizable hanging-paper form and readable letter retained; accepted near-square paper looks coherent. Main tradeoff is much shorter suspension and shifted composition. This is a geometry/proportion change, so visual parity should not be claimed.
